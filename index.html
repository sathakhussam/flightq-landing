Utilization & revenue

Every idle hour is revenue you never get back.
See where the day actually goes.

FlightQ accounts for all twenty-four hours of every aircraft day — flown, outside your operating window, grounded for maintenance, out on lease — and sets what you rostered against what was actually performed, sortie by sortie.

Stop estimating utilization. Start accounting for it.

Most schools quote a number nobody can trace. FlightQ builds it from signed-off sorties, so every hour in the figure has a flight behind it.

  • The 24-hour waterfall: flown, night, maintenance, lease and idle
  • Rostered against performed, by fleet, instructor, tail and simulator

Every cancellation has a cause. Now it has a charge.

A cancelled sortie is a typed, audited record, not a rubbed-out line on a whiteboard.

  • Reasons grouped by schedule, student, instructor and weather
  • Each reason carries its own charge rule and the roles allowed to use it
Cadet progress

See the progress behind the hours.

Hours flown is not progress. FlightQ tracks every cadet against the syllabus they are actually on — mission by mission, graded, with the exercises they had to repeat.

  • Batch journeyCohort hours, flights and milestone completion, month by month.
  • Cadet journeyHours, weekly average, missions flown and milestone, on track or behind.
  • Courses & missionsCourse, milestone and mission, from a reusable mission library.
  • Exercise gradingSatisfactory or repeat, recorded on the sortie and carried into the logbook.
Dispatch & the record

Nothing is billed that was not flown.

Checkout, check-in and sign-off happen in order, enforced on the server. The meter reading that closes a flight is the one that writes the logbook, and the one the invoice is built from — so the three can never drift apart.

  • The dispatch chainNot checked out, checked out, checked in, sign-off pending, completed.
  • Dispatch sheetThe paperwork for the sortie, signed on the last page, on one sheet.
  • The logbook writes itselfFrom sign-offs, in all three places, with simulator time marked.
  • The charge follows the contractPriced by the agreement that was live on the day it flew.

Traceable by design

Ask any number why.

Nothing on a FlightQ screen is typed in. Every figure is built from signed-off sorties, so you can open it and get the flights behind it — the tail, the crew, the times, and why it was flagged.

  • Every figure drills downOpen a deviation and the sorties behind it are listed, worst first.
  • Four angles on the same monthBy fleet, by tail, by instructor and by simulator.
  • Bands and buckets you defineYour own thresholds and ranges, not someone else's idea of normal.
  • Effort beside qualityInstructor load sits next to first-time pass rate, so neither hides the other.

Trust & governance

The right access for every role. A day you can prove you published.

Rules are enforced on the server, not in the browser. Publishing a day takes a snapshot of it, so months later you can still show exactly what the flight line was told to fly.

  • Published snapshotsEvery release of a day is kept, so what the flight line saw is never in doubt.
  • Role-based permissionsSix roles, each seeing and doing only what it should.
  • Enforced on the serverThe checkout chain and conflict rules hold even if the browser is bypassed.
  • Documents served safelyAn uploaded file cannot run script on your origin.
  • One timezone for the schoolToday and the now-line follow where the school actually operates.
